# Homeowners HOBP — Florida — Form HO 0809 02 22 — Amendatory Endorsement Florida

Source: HO 0809 02 22 Amendatory Endorsement Florida.docx (1-page endorsement)
Amends: HO 0003 09 20 Special Form (and presumably earlier base policy editions)
Edition: February 2022

---

## Overview

HO 0809 02 22 is a one-page Florida mandatory amendatory endorsement to the homeowners Special Form (HO 0003). It makes four targeted changes to the base policy, all driven by Florida statutory or regulatory requirements:

1. Innocent insured protection from intentional acts exclusion (domestic abuse)
2. Subrogation carve-out for innocent insured in abuse situations
3. Renewal premium increase notice requirement
4. Suit Against Us substitution (statutory period replaces two-year contractual period)

Note: Earlier version HO 0814 is listed as mandatory in the FL state pages (revision 01 15). HO 0809 02 22 may have superseded HO 0814 with the forms filing approved 01/01/2026. Both are referenced in the source documents.

---

## Amendment 1 — Intentional Acts Exclusion: Innocent Insured Exception (Domestic Abuse)

**What changes:** The Intentional Acts exclusion under Exclusions That Apply To Property Coverages is amended to add an exception for innocent insured victims of domestic abuse.

**Rule:** If the loss is caused by an act of **abuse, as defined by Florida statute**, committed by or at the direction of an insured, the Intentional Acts exclusion does **not** apply to an otherwise covered loss suffered by another insured who:

- Is the subject of the abuse, AND
- Did not cooperate with or contribute to the act that caused the loss, AND
- Either:
  - Files a complaint under the **Florida Protection From Abuse Act** against the abuser (and does not voluntarily dismiss the complaint), OR
  - Seeks a warrant for the abuser's arrest for the act causing the loss and cooperates in prosecution of the abuser

**Payment cap:** Payment to the innocent insured may be limited to that insured's insurable interest in the property, less any payment made to a mortgagee or other party with a legal secured interest.

---

## Amendment 2 — Subrogation: Domestic Abuse Carve-Out

**What changes:** The Subrogation condition (under Policy Conditions, Conditions That Apply To All Coverages) is amended.

**Rule:** An innocent insured who is the subject of abuse, as defined by Florida statute, **cannot waive** his or her right to recover. The company retains all subrogation rights set forth in the Subrogation condition with regard to its right to recover, up to the amount paid, for loss caused by an act of abuse.

**Contrast with base form:** The base form's Subrogation condition allows an insured to waive recovery rights in writing before a loss without affecting coverage. This amendment removes that waiver ability specifically in domestic abuse situations.

---

## Amendment 3 — Renewal Notice Requirement (Added)

**What changes:** A new condition is added under Policy Conditions, Conditions That Apply To All Coverages.

**Rule:** If the company intends to renew the policy subject to a **premium increase**, other than an increase due to a change made at the named insured's request, the company must give the named insured notice at least **30 days** before the renewal is effective.

---

## Amendment 4 — Suit Against Us: Property Coverages (Replaced)

**What changes:** The Suit Against Us condition under Policy Conditions, Conditions Applicable To Property Coverages Only is deleted and replaced.

**New rule:** No suit may be brought against the company unless:
1. All of the terms that apply to the Property Coverages have been complied with, AND
2. The suit is brought within the **time period prescribed by law** for the commencement of such action

**Contrast with base form:** The base HO 0003 09 20 Suit Against Us condition for property coverages uses a **two-year** contractual period after the loss (with fallback to state statutory period if law makes the two-year period invalid). The Florida endorsement removes the two-year period entirely and defers directly to Florida law (currently Florida Statute §95.11 provides a five-year period for written contracts).

**Liability Suit Against Us:** The base form's liability Suit Against Us condition is not amended.
